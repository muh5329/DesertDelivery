Deepening opportunities

Desert Delivery — architecture review

Twelve places where the codebase is shallow: the interface costs almost as much to learn as the implementation saves. Each candidate proposes a deeper module — more behaviour behind a smaller interface, sitting at a seam something actually varies across — chosen for locality (change lands in one file) and testability (the interface becomes the test surface).

CANDIDATE 01 · WORLD

Split Terrain into Ground (the question) and a terrain build (the answer)

world/terrain/terrain.gdworld/island/island.gd:126–142, 681–702 world/life/road_navigation.gd:34–38world/world_manager.gd:44–49
Problem

CONTEXT.md promises Ground is “one question, one answer”. In practice Terrain exposes about thirty distinct members and Island alone touches seventeen of them. Roughly half are a stable query surface (height_at, biome_at, probe, nearest_road); the other half is the generator’s scratch space held public because Island has to fill it before build() — pads, viaducts, roads, road_samples, bridges, heights, mesh_instance.

Two costs follow. First, bridges is published as raw sample-index ranges that exclude the earth ramps, so every consumer re-derives the real deck extent — the same three magic numbers, copied:

// island.gd:685–692 — _gen_aqueducts
while k > 0 and samples[k-1].y - terrain.height_at(...) > 0.25 and b.from - k < 80: k -= 1
k = maxi(k - 3, 0); last_k = mini(last_k + 3, samples.size() - 1)

// road_navigation.gd:34–38 — RoadNavigation.build
while first>0 and samples[first-1].y-terrain.height_at(...)>.25 and int(bridge.from)-first<80: first-=1
for i in range(maxi(first-3,0), mini(last+3, samples.size()-1)):

Two adapters means this is a real seam, not a hypothetical one: the Bridge should answer “where does my deck begin and end” itself.

Second, the leak runs the other way. Terrain.plant_ground_cover — a module whose docstring says it is driven by data/island_map.png — hardcodes island content:

// terrain.gd:876, 892
var town_court := Vector2(300, -245)
var court_road: Vector3 = nearest_road(Vector3(300, 0, -245)).point

That literal is the first entry of Island._plan_town_courtyards’s candidate list (island.gd:1275). Move the Town Square courtyard and grass silently grows back through the paving.

Solution

Two modules where there is one. Ground answers questions — height, normal, biome, density, road distance, nearest Road, probe — and owns nothing mutable. A terrain build consumes Roads, Viaducts and Pads and produces a Ground. bridges becomes a Bridge/Viaduct record with a deck_span() that includes its ramps. Ground-cover exclusions become keep-clear regions the Island hands over, not literals inside Terrain.

Benefits
  • Leverage: Island, RoadNavigation, Autopilot, GunSystem and the HUD all learn one small question surface instead of picking members out of a thirty-member grab-bag.
  • Locality: the ramp rule (0.25 m, 80 samples, 3 of margin) lives in the Bridge. Today changing it means finding both copies.
  • Tests: Ground becomes testable against a synthetic 20×20 map with two Roads and one Viaduct — no generate(), no Terrain3D, no Game. Today life_tests.gd:69–83 can only sample the real island and hope it is representative.

Same file, second finding
height_at silently changes meaning halfway through generation. _t3d_data is assigned at terrain.gd:595, inside _build_terrain3d(), which build() calls at line 423. Before that, height_at is the 3 m bilinear field; after, it is Terrain3D’s 1.5 m cubic resample with per-biome micro relief added. They agree only because of a per-pixel clamp and a smoothstep(3.0, 9.0, rd) road mask — both invisible from the caller’s side. Worth fixing as part of the same split: one name, one meaning.

CANDIDATE 02 · ENTITIES

One GroundDrive behind the Bike and the Truck

entities/vehicles/vehicle.gd:1–69entities/vehicles/bike/bike.gd:225–509 entities/vehicles/truck/truck.gd:210–325tests/riding_feel_tests.gd
Problem

Vehicle is 69 lines, of which apply, place, reset_to_road, set_rider_visible and set_package_visible are all pass. The three methods with bodies — flat_forward, heading, speed_kmh — are overridden by both subclasses anyway, because each tracks yaw in a private _yaw rather than in the transform. Apply the deletion test: deleting Vehicle today moves almost nothing, because every caller already reaches concrete state (bike.airborne, truck.cargo_build_mode, bike.throttle). It is a type tag, not a deep module.

Meanwhile the module that would earn its keep — “given a ControlIntent and a VehicleDefinition, produce speed, yaw, pitch, ground normal, grounded, dust and a sea-reset” — exists twice, near-verbatim. The reverse-engagement latch:

// bike.gd:368–379                          // truck.gd:236–241
_rev_hold += delta                         _rev_hold += delta
if _rev_hold > 0.35 and _brake_repressed:  if _rev_hold > 0.35 and _brake_repressed and not cargo_build_mode:

…and the road recovery, copy-pasted with the comment dropped (bike.gd:498–508 / truck.gd:317–325). Also duplicated: steer smoothing in _read_intent, drag plus the engine-brake fudge, the slope term (* 5.5 on the bike, * 4.5 on the truck — drift, not a decision), the wheel-ray → ground_normal/_pitch block, the head-on collision scrub, _build_dust, and the parked early-out.

ARCHITECTURE.md promises “a car is a new .tres plus a Vehicle subclass, not a fork of the bike.” The Truck demonstrates that today it is a fork of the bike.

Solution

A GroundDrive module the vehicle owns: step(intent, delta) updates speed, yaw, pitch, ground normal, grounded and slip from a VehicleDefinition and a ray set, and owns recover_to_road(). Bike becomes wings and flight on top of it; Truck becomes winch and cargo on top of it. Two adapters already exist, so this is a real seam — and a car really does become a .tres.

Benefits
  • Locality: one place decides what a brake tap does. Today the bike’s tuning has drifted from the truck’s in at least three constants with no record of intent.
  • Tests: riding_feel_tests.gd:194–219 currently drives privates — probe._ray_front.force_raycast_update(), probe._physics_process(DT), probe._air_power = 1.0. The whole brake / reverse / drag / slope table becomes testable with no scene tree and no rays — and the same table runs against the Truck, which today has zero riding-feel coverage.

CANDIDATE 03 · LIFE

Give the Resident a type

world/life/island_life.gd:54–57, 351–366world/life/resident_actor.gd:38–93 ui/hud/resident_catalogue.gd:215–242ui/hud/catalogue/week_grid.gd:11–37 gameplay/journey/journey_system.gd:196–199
Problem

The Resident is the central concept of the newest slice of the game and it exists only as an anonymous Dictionary. Its shape is asserted in one place and read in five:

// island_life.gd:54–57
r.position=r.places.home; r.forward=along; r.route=PackedVector3Array(); r.cursor=0
r.task_key=""; r.activity="sleep"; r.status="At home"; r.moving=false; r.driving=false
r.circuit=0; r.wait=0.0; r.distance=0.0; r.sim_elapsed=0.0; r.speed=0.0
r.surface_normal=Vector3.UP; r.work_progress=0.0; r.completed_tasks=0
r.deliveries_completed=0; r.blocked_time=0.0; r.station_validated=false

Three consequences visible in the code:

  • The interface is discovered by defensive reads. resident_actor.gd hedges — record.get("surface_normal", Vector3.UP), record.get("speed", 0.0) — while line 74 reads record.scale bare. Which keys are guaranteed is stated nowhere.
  • Save and restore each list the fields by hand, and they disagree with creation. save_state persists 14 keys; load_state restores 12, silently dropping activity, blocked_time, station_validated and others. Adding a field to _advance means editing three unrelated lists; forgetting one is silent.
  • world.database.residents = by_id (line 60) is written and never read anywhere in the repo. A seam with zero adapters is not a seam.
Solution

A Resident class owning the record: schedule_for(day), task_at(day, minute), station(place), advance(delta, ground), and one to_dict()/from_dict() pair so persistence is defined once, next to the fields. IslandLife becomes the calendar and the population — not the shape-of-a-person authority.

Benefits
  • Locality: adding a field is one edit, and the save round-trip follows from it rather than being maintained in parallel.
  • Tests: life_tests.gd:110–128 currently swaps the entire population in and out of a live node — life.residents = [crossing_a, crossing_b] … then restores it — to test two cars at a junction. With a Resident type, a junction test constructs two Residents and steps them. No global mutation, no restore-or-corrupt-the-suite hazard.

CANDIDATE 04 · WORLD

A Hub declares its surfaces once, and geometry is derived from that

world/island/island.gd:703–753, 755–797, 829–851, 942–1090 world/kit/world_kit.gd:525–539world/database/hub.gd:33–56
Problem

Every Hub’s walls exist as two independent literals — once in _build_*, once in _define_*:

// island.gd:743 — build side
_stone_wall(hub, Vector2(cx - 22*K, cz + 14*K), Vector2(cx - 4*K, cz + 26*K))
// island.gd:752 — define side, nine lines later
hb_rec.add_wall(Vector2(cx - 22*K, cz + 14*K), Vector2(cx - 4*K, cz + 26*K), 1.0)

The same doubling for the farm, monastery, quarry, Cala Blanca and the Salinas. The Dunes Lookout’s bench is doubled with a hand-computed offset: _build_lookout puts a 0.1-thick plank centred at g + 0.5, _define_lookout records g + 0.55. And Hub.wall_top re-implements _stone_wall’s segment maths from the outside, admitting it in the docstring: “Mirrors how Level builds the wall.”

The telling detail — WorldKit._stone_wall already takes the Hub:

func _stone_wall(parent: Node3D, a: Vector2, b: Vector2, height: float = 1.0, h: Hub = null) -> void:
	if h: h.add_wall(a, b, height)

…and none of the 18 call sites passes it. It cannot work as written: _build_* runs at chunk-load time inside a Recipe, while _define_* must run at generation time — registering a wall from the builder would publish it only when the farm chunk happened to be loaded, which ARCHITECTURE.md principle 7 forbids. So the parameter is a hypothetical seam with zero adapters, and the workaround is hand-mirrored constants.

Solution

Invert it. A Hub declares its surfaces — a list of wall, bench and pad records — at define time, and _build_* derives geometry from that declaration: for w in hb_rec.walls: _stone_wall(hub, w.a, w.b, w.height). wall_top then reads the same record the mesh came from, instead of mirroring the builder.

Benefits
  • Locality: moving a wall is one edit. Today it is two edits nine lines apart, in seven Hubs.
  • Tests: architecture_tests.gd:40 can only assert wall_top(...) != null — it cannot check a tin can actually sits on the mesh, because the mesh exists only once the farm chunk loads. With a single record, drift is measurable: for every Hub, sample wall_top(x) and raycast the built wall in a loaded chunk. A divergence becomes a failing number rather than the pistol quietly missing.

CANDIDATE 05 · STREAMING

A Recipe declares its extent

world/island/island.gd:65–67, 158–175, 553–560, 700, 1330–1335 world/database/world_database.gd:31–37world/streaming/chunk.gd:12–23
Problem

The whole interface is one position and one Callable:

func _at(x: float, z: float, builder: Callable) -> void:
	db.add(x, z, builder)

The unstated invariant is that the geometry the closure builds lies inside the 60 m Chunk containing (x, z). It is violated all over the generator:

  • Aqueducts (line 700) file an arcade spanning the whole Bridge plus both ramps — up to ~160 m of Viaduct — under one Chunk.
  • Hubs (158–175) file at h.centre, but the Villa builds houses, gates and cypresses out to ±62 m: two Chunks away in every direction.
  • Harbour boats (553–560) file at the pier root while the boats sit up to 24 m along it.
  • Town courtyards (1330–1335) file at the centre and build a promenade to path[1] — which _plan_town_courtyards guarantees is at least 32 m away (line 1281).

With chunk_size = 60.0 and stream_radius = 3, the reachable consequence is an aqueduct or a villa wing popping in and out as a Chunk two rings away loads. Nothing in the code — and nothing in architecture_tests.gd, which asserts only record_count > 500 — can detect it.

There is a second unstated rule in the same interface. Chunk.build reseeds kit.rng from the coordinate, so a Recipe calling rng.randf_range inside its closure draws from a different stream than the same call at generation time — deterministic per Chunk, but with the added rule that Recipe order within a Chunk is part of the contract. And two conventions for ground height coexist: _gen_town bakes h := _ground(x, z) at generation and captures it; _gen_limestone calls _ground(x, z) inside the closure at load time.

Solution

_at takes the Recipe’s extent — a radius or an AABB. WorldDatabase.add files it into every Chunk it overlaps (built once, by the first Chunk to claim it) or rejects it. A caller passing a centre for a 160 m arcade is forced to say so.

Benefits
  • Locality: pop-in becomes a property of the Recipe rather than an emergent property of where a closure happened to be filed.
  • Tests: with an extent declared, a test walks every record, builds it into a throwaway node, and asserts the resulting AABB sits inside the Chunk’s bounds plus tolerance — a real regression test against pop-in, impossible to write today.
Before / after

Before — a point that lies

After — an extent the database can see

And a test can now assert that no built Recipe escapes its declared extent.

CANDIDATE 06 · UI / GAMEPLAY

A Panel module for the Counter and the Journal

gameplay/journey/journey_system.gd:82–116ui/hud/resident_catalogue.gd:152–181 core/app/game.gd:246, 254ui/hud/hud.gd:394–397
Problem

The Counter (JourneySystem) and the Journal (ResidentCatalogue) each hand-roll the same five-part protocol: save _old_mouse, set Input.mouse_mode, set player_controls.blocked, latch just_closed + _closed_frame for one frame, and release on the next frame only if the mouse button is up. Game then re-derives “is anything modal” by enumerating both:

// game.gd:254
if rider.is_on_foot() and not catalogue.is_open() and not catalogue.just_closed
   and not journey.is_open() and not journey.just_closed and ...

That is the interface: every new panel obliges an edit here and in hud.gd:397. Two adapters, no seam.

Live bug the duplication is already producing
Opening the Counter pauses delivery handoffs (game.gm.handoffs_paused = true, journey_system.gd:85). Opening the Journal does not — resident_catalogue.gd:152–163 sets blocked and the mouse mode and never touches it. DeliverySystem._process completes a stage after 0.5 s stopped inside a Ring, and reading the Journal is exactly “stopped”. So opening the Journal while parked in a pickup Ring collects the Package behind the book. journey_tests.gd:20 guards precisely this case for the Counter; there is no equivalent for the Journal. The unconditional handoffs_paused = false on close is a second hazard the moment anything else pauses handoffs. Release is asymmetric too: the Counter unblocks only if not catalogue.is_open(); the Journal has no matching check.
Solution

A small Panel stack owning open and close: it captures the mouse mode, blocks controls, pauses handoffs, and holds the one-frame click-through latch. Game asks panels.any_open() instead of naming panels. The Counter and the Journal shrink to layout plus content.

Benefits
  • Leverage: a third panel — a map, a settings screen — is layout only.
  • Locality: the bug above stops being possible; the pause is a property of being open, not something each panel remembers to do.
  • Tests: journey_tests.gd:20–28 and catalogue_view.gd:16, 50–53 currently assert the same latch behaviour twice, once per panel, through a booted Game. One Panel module gets one table-driven test of the protocol; each panel only needs “it opens and shows the right rows”.

CANDIDATE 07 · ENTITIES

Ask the Rider questions, don’t read its Mode

entities/player/rider.gd:16, 22, 62–67gameplay/delivery/delivery_system.gd:188–195 gameplay/journey/journey_system.gd:50, 61–63ui/hud/hud.gd:333–355 core/app/game.gd:161, 209, 254world/life/island_ambience.gd:30
Problem

CONTEXT.md says everyone “reads rider.mode or reacts to mode_changed” — and that is exactly what has gone wrong. Because the enum is the interface, each caller re-derives the policy it actually wanted, differently:

// delivery_system.gd:195 — "can a handover happen", as a mode list
var available := rider == null or rider.mode == Rider.Mode.RIDING or rider.mode == Rider.Mode.DRIVING or rider.mode == Rider.Mode.ON_FOOT
// journey_system.gd:61–63 — "is the courier stopped", with its own thresholds
return game.rider.mode in [Rider.Mode.RIDING, Rider.Mode.DRIVING] and game.rider.vehicle.grounded and absf(game.rider.vehicle.speed) < 2.5

And the same question — who is the courier, and where is he? — is answered independently in four places, two phrased with is_riding() and two with is_on_foot():

game.gd:209             var f: Node3D = rider.vehicle if rider.is_riding() else player
delivery_system.gd:188  var courier: Node3D = player if rider and rider.is_on_foot() else vehicle
journey_system.gd:50    var actor: Node3D = game.player if game.rider.is_on_foot() else game.rider.vehicle
ambience.gd:30          var p := game.player.global_position if game.rider.is_on_foot() else ...

These are equivalent only because Mode has exactly five members today. Add one — IN_TRUCK_BED, CUTSCENE — and they silently split. Worse, the Rider also publishes the raw intent: hud.gd:338 reads rider.last_intent.aim, so a ControlIntent that was supposed to stop at the physics modules is now HUD state.

Solution

Keep the transitions — _apply_mode_effects (rider.gd:177–192) is the genuinely deep part and the Rider owns it well. Replace the enum-as-interface with the questions callers ask: courier() -> Node3D, active_vehicle() -> Vehicle, can_hand_over() -> bool, is_stopped() -> bool, is_aiming() -> bool. mode stays for the debug overlay and the save file.

Benefits
  • Leverage: adding a Mode is a Rider-only change. Today it is a repo-wide grep with silent failure modes.
  • Tests: each predicate becomes directly assertable on a Rider with stubbed bodies. Today the only coverage is feature_tests.gd asserting the enum value after a transition — nothing tests that delivery, journey and ambience agree about who the courier is.

CANDIDATE 08 · LIFE / ENTITIES

One view pool for Residents and wildlife — and let the EntityManager own the distance

world/life/island_life.gd:320–338world/life/island_wildlife.gd:108–122 world/life/resident_actor.gd:112–113entities/entity_manager.gd
Problem

Two modules independently implement the same pattern — hysteresis radius, entities.register, queue_free, unregister:

// island_life.gd:325–338
if distance < 105.0 and r.activity != "sleep": ... entities.register(actor, StringName(r.id), &"resident")

Two adapters means this is a real seam. Meanwhile ARCHITECTURE.md §11–12 says the EntityManager owns exactly this — assigning FULL / REDUCED / ABSTRACT / DORMANT by distance to the focus — and ResidentActor explicitly opts out:

func set_simulation_tier(_tier: int) -> void:

Benefits

  • Tests: there is currently no test of spawn/despawn hysteresis at all — life_tests.gd:97 deliberately picks a Resident without an actor to work around it. A pooled module is testable by feeding synthetic focus positions and asserting register/unregister counts, with no booted island.

CANDIDATE 09 · MAPGEN

A checked contract for the Map — and stop extract.py writing to the runtime path

world/mapgen/extract.py:7, 101world/mapgen/expand.py:22–24, 32–33, 135–139 world/terrain/terrain.gd:21, 25–28, 78–89world/island/island.gd:42–46, 144–151
Problem

The Map contract has five parts, each restated by hand in two or three places: the grid (SIZE 1248 / CELL 3.0 / N 417), the height encoding (r8 / 255 * 90 - 10), the eleven-value Biome order, the painting scale, and the islets format. Nothing checks any of it. The only enforcement is a width check that degrades to nothing:

if err != OK or img.get_width() != N:
	push_error("Terrain: could not load %s (%s); using flat sea" % [MAP_PATH, err])
	_map_h.fill(-6.0); _map_b.fill(Biome.SEA); _map_d.fill(0.0)

And it fires for real: extract.py line 101 writes its 241 px result straight to data/island_map.png — the path Terrain.MAP_PATH requires to be 417 px. Run extract.py alone and the entire world becomes flat sea behind a single push_error; only a subsequent expand.py restores a loadable map. expand.py’s own guard makes it worse: if island_map_720.png is missing it copies whatever is at data/island_map.png and treats it as the pristine original — feeding an already-expanded map back through the K-scale.

Solution

One Map format description both sides read — a small JSON sidecar (grid size, cell, height range, Biome name→index) that expand.py writes and Terrain._load_map validates field-by-field, refusing to boot with a named mismatch instead of a silent flat sea. And extract.py writes to world/mapgen/island_map_720.png — its actual product — so the two stages cannot be run out of order into a broken world.

Benefits
  • Locality: the encoding is defined once and asserted at the seam, rather than transcribed into three files that can drift silently.
  • Tests: there is currently no test that touches the Map at all. A headless test that loads data/island_map.png and asserts its dimensions, that every G value is a valid Biome, that the decoded height range is plausible, and that every islet converts to a position inside the world, turns “the island is flat sea today” from a mystery into one failing line.

Python and GDScript can’t share a source of truth without a generator step, so some duplication is inherent here — the check is the part that earns its keep, not the unification.

CANDIDATE 10 · LIFE

IslandLife.advance_to(minute) instead of a fake save payload

gameplay/journey/journey_system.gd:191–200world/life/island_life.gd:351–366tests/journey_tests.gd:44–47

“Rest until morning” makes time pass by synthesising a save file, per Resident, from a CanvasLayer:

# Use the simulation's restore seam to place everyone at the next morning's station
for person in game.life.residents:
	records[person.id] = {"completed_tasks":..., "deliveries_completed":..., "distance":...}
game.life.load_state({"total_minutes": next_morning, "residents": records, "ambient_time":...})

The comment is honest, which is what makes the friction visible. The interface JourneySystem must know: the exact save schema, that load_state re-runs _assign_task(r, true) and therefore teleports everyone, and — critically — that any field it omits gets reset. That is why it hand-copies three lifetime counters back, and why journey_tests.gd:47 exists only to police that hand-copy. Add a counter to the Resident (candidate 03) and this silently resets it with the test still green.

Solution: IslandLife.advance_to(absolute_minute) — set the clock, reassign every task at the new time, clear in-flight routes, preserve lifetime totals by construction. The Counter calls one line and stops knowing the save format, which is then free to change without breaking a shop button. The test moves down to IslandLife (“skipping 14 hours preserves lifetime totals and places everyone at their 08:00 station”), leaving the journey test to check only that the button is refused away from Villa Rosa.

CANDIDATE 11 · CAMERA

Make Framing the data table it already claims to be

entities/camera/chase_camera.gd:20–29, 132–176tests/riding_feel_tests.gd:274–296

CONTEXT.md calls Framing “a data table”, and the table has six columns — but _chase_update branches on identity, not on data. Seven composed branches plus two as Bike reaches:

var composed := framing == Framing.BIKE                                      // :144
var dist: float = _f.distance + sf * (.7 if composed else 1.1)              // :148
var position_blend := 1.0 - exp(-(8.0 if composed else pos_smooth) * delta)   // :161
fov = lerpf(fov, _f.fov + sf * (4.5 if composed else 7.0), ...)              // :176

Framing.TRUCK was added at line 28 and inherited the “not BIKE” column of every one of those branches by default. Nobody chose 1.1 m of pull-back for a truck; it is what falls out of not composed. A future car or boat gets the same accidental tuning. _target_speed() likewise duck-types (target is Vehicle → .speed, else has_method("speed")) because Player exposes speed as a method and Vehicle as a field.

Have the target satisfy one tiny interface (speed, top_speed) instead of the camera sniffing as Bike. The three existing camera checks then loop over all five Framings from the public follow(), catching the truck’s accidental tuning.

CANDIDATE 12 · CONTROLS

Invert the ControlIntent seam: the controlled thing declares its actions

gameplay/controls/controls.gd:16–57, 78–169entities/player/rider.gd:71–101entities/vehicles/truck/truck.gd:92–102

The seam earns its keep — physics really is identical for the player, the Autopilot and the tests. But the interface is nearly as complex as what sits behind it, and interpretation is smeared across four modules:

  • Keyboard already knows vehicle internals: ctx.wings_out, ctx.airborne, ctx.at_takeoff_speed, ctx.driving_truck, ctx.cargo_build_mode. The Truck’s cargo mode reaches all the way back into the key reader.
  • Rider re-dispatches five edges; Truck.apply re-dispatches five more inside physics, so cargo placement happens on the physics thread via the intent.
  • Scripted.read copies all 21 fields by hand and press() is a string→field switch. Adding one control means six edits at the seam that was supposed to absorb change.

Context is the tell: it exists only because a key’s meaning depends on vehicle state, so vehicle state flows backwards through the seam every tick.

Solution: invert the direction. The thing being controlled declares its action set — Bike and Truck expose control_map(state), or the Rider owns per-Mode maps. Keyboard becomes device→action only; Scripted becomes a generic duplicate() instead of 13 hand-copied lines; edge-triggered commands travel as named commands rather than booleans smuggled through a physics struct. The hand-copy in Scripted.read is exactly the kind of thing that silently drops a field, and nothing asserts its completeness today — a generic copy plus one round-trip test replaces that risk.

Also noticed, not promoted

  • Save / load ordering is a real unwritten invariant. save_manager.gd:50 loads by iterating the file’s key order while save_game writes in registration order (delivery, gun, bike, truck, rider). bike.load_state calls place(), and rider.load_state positions the boy relative to bike.global_position. Reorder the Saves.register calls, or load a save from an older build, and the boy is placed against the bike’s pre-load position — silently. The matching tree-order constraint exists only as a comment at game.gd:90–91. A Saves.register(key, provider, after: [...]) plus one test that loads a shuffled save file would close it. Not promoted only because nothing currently reorders those registrations.
  • Truck escapes its own type. truck.gd:1 declares class_name Truck, and every holder is untyped — var truck in game.gd, rider.gd, bike_audio.gd; Variant in truck_tests.gd. vehicle.gd:52–53 records why the base grew visual hooks — a cold-cache script-compile constraint — but callers still use dynamic dispatch, so we pay for both. And it leaks anyway: hud.gd:349 reads truck.visual.cargo.status_text(), three levels into the renderer. Worth folding into candidate 02 rather than tackling alone.
  • Bike performance travels as set_meta strings. JourneySystem — a UI layer — owns fuel, engine level and cargo mass and publishes them by stamping three string keys on the Bike node, read back in bike.gd and re-clamped independently in hud.gd. The HUD reads only the Bike, so the gauges are wrong while driving the Truck. A typed loadout value would fix it; bike.performance_profile() itself is genuinely deep and should stay.
  • RoadNavigation passes the deletion test — build/support/ path over bucketed A*, bridge-deck detection and lane offsetting. Its one wart is that path() takes lane: float and infers pedestrian-vs-traffic from lane > 2.0, an unexpressed invariant shared with island_life.gd:118. A named lane enum fixes it — a small change, not a restructure.
  • Earning their keep, leave alone: Hub (answers with the chunk unloaded, exactly as principle 7 promises), IslandArt (62 lines of caching plus material rewriting behind prop_parts), _scatter_biome (eight tunables behind one call, used eleven times), and IslandLife._advance — long, but genuinely dense, with no pure functions extracted for testability’s sake.
